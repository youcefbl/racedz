A running watch turns "I went for a run" into real data: pace, distance, heart rate, and a record you can actually improve on. You don't need the most expensive model — you need the right one for how you train. Here are the picks that make sense in Algeria, from cheapest to premium.

<Callout type="tip" title="The one feature that matters most">
**Built-in GPS.** A watch with its own GPS tracks your route and distance without your phone. Cheaper smartbands borrow your phone's GPS instead — fine to start, but you'll want the real thing once you're chasing a time.
</Callout>

## Best absolute budget: a smartband

<ProductPick name="Xiaomi Smart Band 9" badge="Phone-assisted start" where="Check Algerian electronics retailers" url="https://www.mi.com/global/product/xiaomi-smart-band-9/specs/">
Xiaomi's published specifications list an optical heart-rate sensor and up to 21 days of typical use, but no positioning sensor. Treat route and pace recording as phone-dependent, and remember that wrist heart-rate data is an estimate rather than medical data.
</ProductPick>

## Best budget GPS watch

<ProductPick name="Amazfit Bip 5" badge="Entry GPS option" where="Import / online order" url="https://us.amazfit.com/products/amazfit-bip-5">
The manufacturer lists four satellite-positioning systems, a 1.91-inch screen, and up to 10 days of typical use. Positioning accuracy still depends on buildings, terrain, and satellite conditions.
</ProductPick>

## Best value — the runner's sweet spot

<ProductPick name="Coros Pace 3" badge="Strong training value" where="Import / online order" url="https://us.coros.com/pace3">
COROS lists optional dual-frequency positioning, breadcrumb navigation, 15 days of regular use, and 38 hours of full GPS use. It is a strong shortlist candidate for runners who prioritize battery life and training tools, but compare its app and navigation with your needs.
</ProductPick>

## Best mid-range Garmin

<ProductPick name="Garmin Forerunner 165" where="Import / online order" url="https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/1055469/">
Garmin lists an AMOLED display, built-in GPS, structured workouts, recovery time, and up to 11 days in smartwatch mode. It does not have the multi-band GPS or training-readiness feature listed for the Forerunner 265.
</ProductPick>

## Premium — for the data lover

<ProductPick name="Garmin Forerunner 265" badge="Deeper training data" where="Import / online order" url="https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/886785/">
Compared with the 165, Garmin lists multi-band GPS, training readiness, training status, and multisport activity support. Those extras are useful only if you will act on them; they are not necessary for logging everyday runs.
</ProductPick>

## Which should you buy?

| You are… | Buy |
| --- | --- |
| Just starting, unsure you'll stick with it | Xiaomi Smart Band 9 |
| Committed, want GPS without breaking the bank | Amazfit Bip 5 |
| Training seriously and want the best value | **Coros Pace 3** |
| Invested in Garmin's ecosystem | Forerunner 165 / 265 |

For most runners, start with the least expensive device that records the data you will actually use. Prices, warranty coverage, and Algerian availability change quickly, so compare the current local or import listing before paying.
