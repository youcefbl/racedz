Runners often add more kilometres when they want to improve. Sometimes the missing piece is not another run, but enough strength to handle the running already on the plan. Research suggests that high-load or combined strength programs can produce small to moderate improvements in running economy or performance for some middle- and long-distance runners. It is useful support work—not a shortcut and not a guarantee against injury.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep the goal simple">
Two short, consistent sessions that preserve good form are more useful than one exhausting gym day that disrupts your running week.
</Callout>

## The five movement patterns

You do not need a complicated exercise library. Build each session around these patterns:

| Pattern | Beginner option | Progression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | Bodyweight box squat | Goblet squat |
| Single-leg | Supported split squat | Rear-foot-elevated split squat |
| Hinge | Hip hinge with a stick | Romanian deadlift |
| Calf | Two-leg calf raise | Single-leg calf raise |
| Trunk | Short side plank | Longer side plank or carry |

Start with movements you can control through a comfortable range. Load is useful only when position and tempo remain stable.

## A simple two-day routine

### Day A

1. Goblet squat — 2–3 sets of 6–10 controlled repetitions
2. Romanian deadlift — 2–3 sets of 6–10
3. Calf raise — 2–3 sets of 8–15
4. Side plank — 2 sets of 20–40 seconds per side

### Day B

1. Split squat — 2–3 sets of 6–10 per side
2. Step-up — 2–3 sets of 6–10 per side
3. Bent-knee calf raise — 2–3 sets of 8–15
4. Suitcase carry — 2–3 controlled walks per side

For the first two weeks, use bodyweight or a light load and leave several good repetitions in reserve. Add a small amount of weight or one repetition only when every set stays technically sound. There is no need to train to failure.

## Where it fits in a running week

Place strength after an easy run or later on the same day as a harder session so that recovery days remain easy. Avoid a demanding leg session immediately before intervals, a long run, or a race. A sample week could look like this:

- **Monday:** easy run + Day A
- **Tuesday:** rest or easy mobility
- **Wednesday:** quality run
- **Thursday:** easy run + Day B
- **Friday:** rest
- **Saturday:** long run
- **Sunday:** easy run or rest

This is an example, not a universal prescription. Reduce the gym volume during race week or when running volume rises sharply.

## What soreness is acceptable?

Mild, short-lived muscle soreness can happen when an exercise is new. Sharp pain, worsening joint pain, swelling, altered walking, or pain that changes your running form is a reason to stop and reassess. If you are returning from injury or managing a health condition, a qualified clinician or coach should adapt the plan to you.

<Callout type="warning" title="Strength does not make you injury-proof">
Injury risk depends on training load, recovery, previous injury, sleep, health, terrain, and many other factors. Strength training may support capacity, but it cannot erase a poorly progressed running plan.
</Callout>

## How to know it is working

Judge the plan across weeks, not by next-day soreness. Useful signs include smoother control under load, gradual strength gains, and no loss of quality in key runs. Running economy changes are usually measured in a lab, so everyday runners should focus on consistent training and performance trends rather than trying to feel an immediate efficiency boost.
